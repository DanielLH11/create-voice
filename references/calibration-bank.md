# Calibration bank

Dimensions a corpus cannot settle on its own, each resolved by showing variants and
asking which one is them. Recognition is reliable; self-description is not, so never
convert one of these into an open question ("how do you handle transitions?").

## How to run a battery

Ask with `AskUserQuestion`, one dimension per question, `preview` carrying the
variant text so the options sit side by side in monospace. Three variants, never
more. Offer no "it depends" option: the person will take it every time. If they
genuinely split by register, the answer is two runs of the same battery, one per
register, not a fourth option.

Write variants in the target language, and seed them from the person's own material
once you have it: same topic, same facts, only the dimension under test changing.
The examples below are English scaffolding, not text to paste.

Log every answer. A dimension nobody answered is a dimension the profile must not
claim to know.

## Dimensions

**1. Sentence rhythm.** Same content at three spreads.
- A: even, every sentence 12 to 18 words.
- B: bursty, a 4-word sentence against a 30-word one.
- C: long, one sentence carrying three clauses.

**2. Opening move.** How a message starts.
- A: cold open, straight into the claim.
- B: one line of context, then the claim.
- C: a question, then the answer.

**3. Assertion strength.**
- A: flat claim, no hedge.
- B: hedged ("I think", "probably", "might").
- C: claim plus the condition under which it fails.

**4. Structure.**
- A: unbroken prose.
- B: bullets.
- C: prose with one bulleted list inside it.

**5. Transitions.**
- A: explicit connectors (so, but, because).
- B: juxtaposition, sentences set next to each other.
- C: paragraph breaks doing the work.

**6. Closing.**
- A: summary of what was said.
- B: a question or a next step.
- C: stops on the last point, no closer.

**7. Disagreement.** The highest-signal dimension. Same objection, three temperatures.
- A: direct contradiction.
- B: concedes a point first, then disagrees.
- C: asks a question that exposes the problem.

**8. Humor.**
- A: dry, one understated line.
- B: self-deprecating.
- C: none, straight through.

**9. Technical density.** Explaining the same thing to a non-expert.
- A: correct jargon, unexplained.
- B: jargon with a gloss.
- C: analogy, jargon avoided.

**10. Frustration.** What leaks when something is broken.
- A: flat and clipped.
- B: explicit ("this is a mess").
- C: sarcastic.

**11. Self-reference.** How much "I" appears.
- A: first person throughout.
- B: impersonal, the subject does the acting.
- C: second person, addressing the reader.

**12. Length instinct.** Given something worth 300 words.
- A: writes 120 and stops.
- B: writes 300.
- C: writes 600 and cuts nothing.

## Confirming corpus findings

Crutch phrases come out of the script, not this bank, and get confirmed one by one:

> "`en plan` shows up 47 times in the export. Three readings: it is how you talk
> everywhere, it is how you talk to family only, or it is noise from this
> conversation. Which one?"

Then the second question, which matters more:

> "Should an agent writing as you reproduce it, or is it a tic you would cut if you
> saw it in your own draft?"

A phrase only reaches the profile after both answers. Frequency alone proves the
habit exists, never that they want it.
